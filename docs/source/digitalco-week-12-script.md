# DigitalCo Week 12: The Reckoning of Cost
## The Lock-In Detonates Again, Data Gravity, and the Did-They-Learn Re-Test

Week 12 follows the five-beat shape the earlier weeks established, so this script applies the pattern rather than re-explaining it. It opens Phase 4, the reckoning and synthesis stretch, and it brings the Week 4 lock-in home a second time as the cloud bill arrives at the scale of the whole connected fleet. The week is built on a cost curve that bends the wrong way, data gravity pulling the bill upward faster than the fleet grows, and it carries a deliberate echo, because the same committed-spend seduction from Week 4 returns to ask whether the team learned anything in the eight weeks since. It's the clearest test in the sim of whether a team can recognize a trap it's already fallen into once.

---

# The cold open

The cloud bill has become the largest line in the IT budget and it's growing faster than the fleet that drives it. Every connected machine sends telemetry to the cloud, the data accumulates rather than passing through, and storage and egress and compute compound on top of the AI workloads added in Week 9, so the bill scales with the strategy's success, which is the perverse shape of it, that the more the connected fleet grows the heavier the cost becomes. The Week 4 sourcing decision and the Week 7 squeeze come fully home here, with a team that took the sweet deal and never hedged now trapped under an unmanageable and accelerating bill, and a team that negotiated portability and began hedging in Week 7 holding real options and real leverage.

Sensing the team's pain, the hyperscaler makes an offer, a committed-spend discount, a lower rate in exchange for a larger multi-year commitment, and it lands like relief because it lowers the bill today. It is also the exact same trap as the Week 4 sweet deal wearing a slightly different suit, lower cost now in exchange for a deeper, longer cage, and the whole week turns partly on whether the team recognizes the move it's already seen. The task is to resolve the infrastructure architecture for the fleet at scale, how much to push to the edge, whether to repatriate workloads, how to control the bill, and what to do about the hyperscaler relationship, and the test underneath it is whether the team reaches for the architecture that fixes the problem or the discount that defers it.

---

# The state of play

The discovery phase puts the cloud bill in front of the team in detail, and the detail is the diagnosis. The cost breakdown shows where the money goes, the egress and storage and compute and the AI workloads, and the growth curve shows the bill outrunning the fleet, and the data-gravity artifact shows why, that the telemetry piles up in the cloud and can't easily move once it's there, so the cost compounds and the lock-in deepens with every passing month. The team's Week 4 terms and Week 7 hedge status determine its room to maneuver, with a hedged team able to renegotiate from strength and a trapped team able only to absorb or commit further, and the FinOps view shows what disciplined cost management could and couldn't recover.

The options are genuinely different in whether they fix the problem or feed it. Taking the committed-spend discount lowers the bill now and deepens the lock-in, the relief that's also the cage, and it's the move a team reaches for when it treats the bill as a price to negotiate rather than a curve to bend. Pushing processing to the edge, on the machines and at the customer sites, reduces the telemetry sent to the cloud and breaks the cost curve at its source, the real fix. Repatriating some workloads to private infrastructure controls cost where the cloud's economics stopped making sense. Renegotiating or restructuring the hyperscaler relationship is available from strength if the team hedged and only from weakness if it didn't. And FinOps discipline manages what's left. A team that grasps data gravity reaches for the edge and the architecture, while a team that doesn't reaches for the discount and finds the bill back, larger, a year later, with less freedom to do anything about it. The Week 9 vendor concentration compounds underneath all of it, because a team that put its AI on the same hyperscaler deepened exactly the dependence this week exposes.

# The executive and board reads

Reinhardt is the pull toward the discount, and as in Week 4 it's because he's half right. The bill is genuinely a problem and the discount genuinely lowers it, so his cost instinct lands on the relief, and a team that over-weights him talks itself into the committed-spend trap on his reasoning a second time, which is the precise repeat the week is designed to catch. The way to satisfy Reinhardt correctly is to show him the edge-and-architecture fix that bends the curve durably rather than the discount that bends it temporarily, which is a harder argument and the right one, and a team that's been converting him across the sim can make it land.

The rest of the reads point toward the durable fix. Chen wants the data-services bet's infrastructure to be sustainable rather than a runaway cost, because an advantage that costs more every quarter isn't much of an advantage, so the strategic read aligns with the architecture rather than the discount. Calloway watches the reckoning as one more signal of whether this CIO manages consequences or defers them, and the board reads the cost crisis as a test of whether the strategy can stand on its own economics. The week rewards a team that treats the bill as a problem to be engineered rather than negotiated, and it punishes a team that reaches, again, for the convenient deal.

# The advisors in Week 12

This is the week Frank Delgado and Marcus Webb lead together for the third time, and Frank's role is the conscience of the whole lock-in thread. He reads the bill he warned about in Week 4, larger now because the fleet grew, and he names the committed-spend discount for exactly what it is, "this is the same trap as the sweet deal, lower bill now and deeper cage later, so the only question that matters this week is whether we learned anything." He's not gloating, he's testing, and a team that hears him recognizes the move it's seen before. Marcus supplies the fix Frank's warning implies, that the bill grows because the team sends everything to a cloud where the data has gravity, and that pushing processing to the edge and repatriating what makes sense and disciplining the rest breaks the curve at its source, "that's the real fix, not a discount," and he notes that the edge architecture is better for the data-services strategy anyway because processing at the source means faster insights.

The others sharpen the edges. Diane holds the learning frame directly, that the team is standing in front of the same choice it made in Week 4 and that what it does here tells her whether it's been paying attention, which is the coherence conscience turned toward the meta-lesson of learning across the arc. Daniel keeps the strategy in view, warning against breaking the data-services bet to fix the bill and pointing out that the edge fix serves the strategy rather than compromising it. Renata holds the through-line from the infrastructure side, noting that edge devices and repatriated workloads are their own attack surface and have to be secured rather than bolted on, the security lesson following the architecture as it has all along. Zoe is lighter on emerging edge and infrastructure approaches with the hype discounted. The team's job is to break the cost curve with architecture, refuse or consciously bound the committed-spend trap, renegotiate from whatever strength its earlier hedging earned, and keep the strategy and the security intact, which together amount to demonstrating that the Week 4 lesson finally took.

# The decision

The team submits its infrastructure architecture and its hyperscaler decision, with the deliverable scaling to the tier, an executive memo at the graduate level that reasons to the architecture and the cost strategy and a structured analysis at the undergraduate level that applies the named lenses, cloud economics, data gravity, edge versus cloud, FinOps, and repatriation. The submission carries the infrastructure architecture, how much to push to the edge, what to repatriate, and the cost-curve-breaking strategy, made with data gravity in view. It carries the hyperscaler decision, whether to take the committed-spend discount, renegotiate or restructure from strength, or hedge further. And it carries the FinOps discipline for managing the cost going forward.

The tempting moves sit in front of the team unlabeled, and the central one is the discount it's seen before. Taking the committed-spend discount lowers the bill today and deepens the lock-in, the same trap as Week 4, and a team that reaches for it is failing the did-they-learn test in the most direct way, choosing relief over fix a second time. Centralizing everything and treating the bill as a negotiation rather than an architecture is the data-gravity trap, the unmanageable curve left unbent. And staying trapped, for a team that never hedged, is the Week 4 cost fully come home with no options to soften it. The sounder path is edge-and-architecture choices that break the cost curve, a renegotiation from whatever strength the earlier hedging earned, a refusal of the committed-spend trap or a conscious bounding of it, and FinOps discipline, all of it coherent with the strategy and demonstrating that the lock-in lesson took. The students experience these as open, and the scoring teaches that recognizing a trap you've already fallen into is the whole skill of the week.

# Scoring Week 12

The submission runs across the four dimensions, and this week leans on Strategic Judgment and the cloud-economics reasoning inside it, with the lock-in payoff landing in Consequence Management. Strategic Judgment rewards edge-and-architecture choices that break the cost curve and a right-sized edge-versus-cloud split, and it penalizes the data-gravity trap and the committed-spend re-trap. Consequence Management scores the handling of the hyperscaler relationship and the cost crisis, and this is where the Week 4 and Week 7 choices fully pay off or punish, with a hedged team renegotiating from strength and a trapped team absorbing a bill it can't control. Coherence scores whether the infrastructure decision fits the strategy and the earlier choices, and it carries the did-they-learn dimension, because a team that breaks the curve demonstrates the lock-in lesson took while a team that takes the discount again demonstrates it didn't. Deliverable Quality rewards clean application of the cloud-economics frameworks at the undergraduate tier and analytical depth on the infrastructure economics at the graduate tier.

The trap detection lives inside these four, with the committed-spend discount scoring as a Strategic Judgment hit and functioning as the did-they-learn test, the data-gravity trap scoring as a Strategic Judgment hit for the unmanaged curve, and the Week 4 and Week 7 lock-in detonation landing as a Consequence Management hit and a real budget drain for a trapped team. The did-they-learn dimension is the week's distinctive feature, because a team that fell for the sweet deal in Week 4 and reaches for the discount again here has shown, in the clearest possible way, that consequence didn't teach it, while a team that fell for the sweet deal in Week 4 and refuses the discount now has shown that it did, which is a meta-lesson the Week 14 debrief surfaces explicitly.

The advisors say the same things in the same voices to both, and the difference is only in how much the wrapper helps the team recognize a trap it's already fallen into once.
