# DigitalCo: The Deliverable Rubrics for the Ten Lighter Weeks
## Paired Undergraduate and Graduate Grading Sheets for Weeks 2 through 12

These rubrics complete the assessment side, covering the ten weeks that carry lighter deliverables, and they inherit the architecture the heavy-four rubrics established rather than restating it. The same four dimensions are scored in the same three bands with the same tier logic, the undergraduate version tilting toward Deliverable Quality and the framework application inside it and the graduate version toward Strategic Judgment under ambiguity and Coherence. What changes from the heavy weeks is the weight and the scale of the artifact, since each lighter week asks for a shorter memo at the graduate tier or a focused framework worksheet at the undergraduate tier rather than a full strategic document. And what changes from week to week is which dimension leads, set by what the week is teaching, which is the one design choice each rubric below turns on.

One boundary carries over unchanged. These rubrics grade the deliverable and feed the accumulating four-dimension totals, but they don't enforce the gates. The security and OT exposure, the budget credibility, and the board's verdict all operate at the scenario level and cap the endgame tier regardless of how polished any single artifact was, so the rubric measures the work and the scenario measures the consequences, exactly as in the heavy weeks.

# Week 2 — The alignment confrontation

The deliverable is the response to the executive confrontation, a navigation of the pushback against the Week 1 direction that either holds the thesis or adjusts it. The undergraduate version applies the stakeholder-alignment and change-management lenses with the frameworks visible, and the graduate version is an executive memo that handles the confrontation and reasons to its position unaided. This is the week the coherence dimension goes to work for the first time, so Coherence leads. Strong work holds the Week 1 anchor under pressure and adjusts tactically without abandoning the thesis, giving Calloway the cover the earlier read warranted where it warranted it, while Weak work either caves to the loudest voice and contradicts the direction it set a week earlier or refuses to bend at all, which are the two ways the dimension fails. Strategic Judgment scores reading the confrontation's substance apart from its politics, and Consequence Management scores the handling of Calloway and the executive who pushed.

The undergraduate weighting puts Deliverable Quality around thirty with Coherence and Strategic Judgment near twenty-five each and Consequence Management filling the rest, and the graduate weighting puts Coherence first near thirty-five because the anchor is the whole test this week, with Strategic Judgment and Consequence Management splitting most of the remainder and Deliverable Quality lighter.

# Week 3 — The failing project

The deliverable is the recommendation on the troubled project, a kill, rescue, or restructure call with the budget plan behind it. The undergraduate version applies the sunk-cost and project-governance lenses, and the graduate version reasons to the call in an executive memo. Strategic Judgment leads, and the sunk-cost reasoning inside it is the test. Strong work sets the money already spent aside and judges on forward value, and where it takes the integrator's lifeline it does so with open eyes about the lock-in that accelerator carries, while Weak work throws good money after bad in the classic sunk-cost trap or grabs the integrator accelerator blind to the fuse it lights for later weeks. Consequence Management scores the Reinhardt conversion, the single largest relationship swing in the sim, and the budget credibility the plan does or doesn't build, and Coherence scores whether the call fits the Week 1 priorities.

The undergraduate weighting leans on Strategic Judgment and Deliverable Quality near thirty each with Consequence Management and Coherence filling the rest, and the graduate weighting puts Strategic Judgment first near thirty-five with Consequence Management rising to twenty-five because the gate and the relationship both turn here, and Coherence and Deliverable Quality lighter.

# Week 4 — The foundation

The deliverable is the sourcing and architecture decision, the make-buy-rent and core-versus-context calls with the platform terms attached. The undergraduate version applies the make-buy-rent, core-versus-context, and total-cost-of-ownership lenses, and the graduate version is an executive memo. Strategic Judgment leads, with Coherence close behind, because the test is whether the team builds what differentiates and rents what doesn't while keeping the terms lock-in-aware. Strong work builds the differentiator, buys or rents the context, and negotiates portability and exit into the platform terms, while Weak work takes the sweet deal as written and sets the deepest lock-in in the sim, builds everything and starves the differentiator on undifferentiated plumbing, or buys the very thing that was supposed to set the company apart. Coherence scores whether the sourcing serves the Week 1 thesis, and Consequence Management scores the vendor relationship and the cost second-order effects the total-cost view should surface.

The undergraduate weighting splits Deliverable Quality and Strategic Judgment near thirty each with Coherence and Consequence Management filling the rest, and the graduate weighting puts Strategic Judgment and Coherence near thirty each, since the sourcing judgment and its fit to the spine are the week, with Consequence Management and Deliverable Quality lighter.

# Week 5 — The read

The deliverable is the read on the competitive signal, a chase, hold, or conviction call on the rival's move with the recommendation that follows. The undergraduate version applies the competitive-analysis and signal-versus-noise lenses, and the graduate version is an executive memo. Strategic Judgment leads, and the test is conviction against the herd and signal against hype. Strong work holds conviction where the Week 1 thesis warrants it and tells the real market signal apart from Zoe's enthusiasm, declining to chase Meridian reflexively just because a rival moved, while Weak work abandons the thesis to follow the herd or takes the hype as though it were signal. Coherence scores whether the read holds the anchor rather than bending it to the news of the week, and Consequence Management scores the handling of the board and executive expectations the rival's move stirred up.

The undergraduate weighting leans on Deliverable Quality and Strategic Judgment near thirty each with Coherence and Consequence Management filling the rest, and the graduate weighting puts Strategic Judgment first near thirty-five with Coherence near twenty-five, since holding the line under competitive noise is the judgment being tested, and Consequence Management and Deliverable Quality lighter.

# Week 6 — The platform question

The deliverable is the platform decision, whether to build a platform at all and how open to make it, with the openness posture stated. The undergraduate version applies the platform-strategy lens and the Predix case, and the graduate version is an executive memo. Strategic Judgment leads, with Coherence close, because the test is sizing the platform ambition to the actual differentiator and resolving the openness question on purpose rather than by accident. Strong work matches the platform to the differentiator the team actually holds and settles the data-rights posture consciously, while Weak work builds a platform on sand in a fit of platform envy with no differentiator to anchor it, the Predix trap exactly, or opens the platform without resolving who owns the data, the quiet landmine that pre-loads the Week 11 crisis. Coherence carries extra freight here because this is the first week a structured prior decision constrains the current one, so a platform that contradicts the Week 4 differentiator takes a real hit, and Consequence Management scores the channel signal Ferraro raises.

The undergraduate weighting splits Deliverable Quality and Strategic Judgment near thirty each with Coherence and Consequence Management filling the rest, and the graduate weighting puts Strategic Judgment and Coherence near thirty each, since the platform judgment and its fit to the Week 4 choice are the test, with Consequence Management and Deliverable Quality lighter.

# Week 7 — The squeeze

The deliverable is the response to the vendor squeeze, a renegotiate, switch, absorb, or hedge call with the handling of the OT signal alongside it. The undergraduate version applies the vendor-management, switching-cost, and OT-risk lenses, and the graduate version is an executive memo. Consequence Management leads, because this is where the Week 4 lock-in first comes due and the quality of the response is a function of the terms the team set earlier. Strong work answers the squeeze from whatever negotiating room the Week 4 terms left, begins the hedge that pays off in Week 12, and addresses the OT signal so the gate stays survivable, while Weak work switches vendors reflexively in the move that is itself the trap, the costliest path available, or misses the OT signal entirely and lets the gate close, which caps the endgame below the top tier no matter how the week itself reads. Strategic Judgment scores the squeeze response on its merits, and Coherence scores whether it fits the Week 4 sourcing. The OT gate does its own separate work on the endgame ceiling here, which the rubric leaves alone, since the rubric grades the artifact and the gate caps the tier.

The undergraduate weighting puts Consequence Management first near thirty with Deliverable Quality and Strategic Judgment near twenty-five each and Coherence filling the rest, and the graduate weighting puts Consequence Management first near thirty with Strategic Judgment and Coherence near twenty-five each and Deliverable Quality lighter.

# Week 9 — The bet

The deliverable is the AI investment decision, the predictive-maintenance bet with the handling of the shadow-AI incident attached. The undergraduate version applies the AI-strategy, build-versus-buy, and governance lenses, and the graduate version is an executive memo. Strategic Judgment leads, and the week carries a deliberate reversal the rubric has to honor, because the board is right this week and the trap is not board-alignment but AI theater. Strong work makes the board-aligned predictive-maintenance bet the foundation actually supports and governs the shadow-AI incident before it becomes a fuse, while Weak work chases a flashy demonstration with no foundation under it, which lands especially hard if the Week 8 data strategy left nothing to build on, or lets the shadow-AI incident run ungoverned into the weeks ahead. Consequence Management scores the vendor-concentration effect, since putting the AI on the same hyperscaler deepens the dependence the Week 12 reckoning exposes, and the handling of the incident, and Coherence scores whether the bet extends the Week 8 data foundation.

The undergraduate weighting leans on Deliverable Quality and Strategic Judgment near thirty each with Consequence Management and Coherence filling the rest, and the graduate weighting puts Strategic Judgment first near thirty-five, since telling real substance from theater under a correct board is the judgment being tested, with Coherence near twenty-five and Consequence Management and Deliverable Quality lighter.

# Week 10 — The breach

The deliverable is the incident response, an executive crisis memo and response plan that covers containment, disclosure, the ransom decision, and the triage of the compound crisis. The undergraduate version applies the incident-response-lifecycle, OT and IT security, crisis-communication, and disclosure lenses, and the graduate version is an executive crisis memo with no scaffolding. Consequence Management leads, because the week is fundamentally a crisis-handling test. Strong work triages the breach and the leak and the resignation and the deadline in parallel rather than in sequence, discloses transparently now that concealment is impossible, and reasons soundly to a ransom decision, while Weak work handles the crisis linearly and gets overwhelmed, spins or hides the breach after it has already gone public, or reaches a ransom decision thoughtlessly in either direction. The ransom call is graded on the reasoning rather than the choice, since a well-argued payment and a well-argued refusal are both defensible, and that reasoning is the central human judgment of the week. Strategic Judgment scores the decisions under pressure, and the OT gate does its separate work, scaling the blast radius from the team's posture and enforcing the ceiling that was set in Week 7, which the rubric leaves to the scenario.

The undergraduate weighting puts Consequence Management first near thirty with Deliverable Quality and Strategic Judgment near twenty-five each and Coherence filling the rest, and the graduate weighting puts Consequence Management first near thirty with Strategic Judgment and Coherence near twenty-five each and Deliverable Quality lighter.

# Week 11 — The reckoning of trust

The deliverable is the data-rights resolution, the public-pressure response, and the trust-repair plan, the hardest single call in the sim because three earlier threads converge on it. The undergraduate version applies the data-rights, privacy-by-design, and stakeholder-trust lenses, and the graduate version is an executive memo. Strategic Judgment leads, with Consequence Management close behind, because the test is finding the shared-value middle where both extremes lose and then repairing the channel from it. Strong work reaches the shared-value resolution that preserves both the advantage and the trust, repairs the relationship through Ferraro, and settles the lawsuit where settling serves, while Weak work holds firm and wins the rights at the cost of the customers or concedes everything and surrenders the strategy to buy peace, the two losing poles of the symmetry trap. Coherence scores whether the resolution preserves the data-services strategy, and it carries a subtlety the grader has to weigh, since a team extending a shared-value position it already held earns full credit while a team pivoting to the same answer from an earlier land grab carries a credibility problem that caps the repair, so the identical resolution can deserve different marks.

The undergraduate weighting splits Strategic Judgment near thirty with Deliverable Quality and Consequence Management near twenty-five each and Coherence filling the rest, and the graduate weighting puts Strategic Judgment first near thirty with Coherence and Consequence Management near twenty-five each and Deliverable Quality lighter.

# Week 12 — The reckoning of cost

The deliverable is the infrastructure architecture and the hyperscaler decision with the FinOps discipline behind it. The undergraduate version applies the cloud-economics, data-gravity, edge-versus-cloud, and FinOps lenses, and the graduate version is an executive memo. Strategic Judgment leads, with Consequence Management close, because the test is breaking the cost curve with architecture rather than negotiating the bill, and recognizing a trap the team may have fallen for once already. Strong work pushes processing to the edge and repatriates what makes sense to bend the curve at its source, refuses the committed-spend discount that is the Week 4 sweet deal in a new suit, and renegotiates from whatever strength the earlier hedge earned, while Weak work takes the committed-spend discount and fails the did-they-learn test in the most direct way or centralizes everything and leaves the data-gravity curve unbent. Coherence carries the did-they-learn dimension directly, since a team that breaks the curve demonstrates the lock-in lesson finally took while a team that reaches for the discount again demonstrates it didn't, and Consequence Management scores where the Week 4 and Week 7 choices fully pay off or punish.

The undergraduate weighting splits Strategic Judgment near thirty with Deliverable Quality and Consequence Management near twenty-five each and Coherence filling the rest, and the graduate weighting puts Strategic Judgment first near thirty with Consequence Management and Coherence near twenty-five each and Deliverable Quality lighter.

# The assessment side is complete

With these ten the rubrics now cover all fourteen weeks, the heavy four at full weight and the lighter ten scaled to match. Every week scores the same four dimensions in the same three bands with the same tier logic, and the only thing a grader carries from week to week is which dimension leads and where the scenario, rather than the rubric, is doing the work, the gates and the board verdict capping the endgame regardless of any single artifact's polish.
